The short version

I am working towards methods -- educational, technological, personal -- for keeping seagrass science, and the people doing it, afloat

My dissertation research uses multibeam echosounders, the same acoustic technology used to map shipwrecks and the seafloor, to "see" seagrass canopy structure in water too murky for satellites or aerial photos to read clearly. I built the multibeam seagrass mapping protocol currently used at COMIT, mounted on an autonomous surface vessel I helped develop from the ground up.

I'm a mom of two living in an inland agricultural community, where I bring ocean science to kids. I believe that science needs everyone, and that curiosity and wonder for the world are two of the most important things we can grow right now, along with healthy soil, grasses, and biodiversity.
